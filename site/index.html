Open-source Zigbee hub

Your own Zigbee hub, on one small ESP32 board

ZHAC turns an Espressif ESP32 board into a complete Zigbee hub. The radio, the device library, your automations and the web interface all run on the board. No Raspberry Pi, no Docker, no cloud account.

From the box to your first automation

Five steps with the ESP32-S31 board. No toolchain to install.

  1. 1

    Get the board and two cables

    A USB-C cable that carries data (charge-only cables will not work) and an Ethernet cable to your router.

  2. 2

    Flash it from your browser

    Open zhac.org/flash in Chrome or Edge, pick your board and click Install. It takes about two minutes.

  3. 3

    Plug in Ethernet and open the hub

    Go to http://zhac.local. Within ten minutes of switching it on, set the admin password — that keeps strangers on your network out.

  4. 4

    Pair your first device

    Click Add a device, then put the sensor, plug or bulb in pairing mode. It appears in the list with its controls.

  5. 5

    Make it do something

    Create a rule from a recipe, such as light on with motion. From then on it runs on the hub by itself.

Flashing, step by step

Everything happens in the browser. Nothing to install except, on Windows, sometimes a USB driver.

checklistBefore you start

  • Chrome or Edge on a desktop computer — Safari and Firefox cannot talk to USB devices.
  • A USB-C cable that carries data.
  • On Windows, if the board does not show up: install the CP210x USB driver.
  • Close other programs that use the serial port, like the Arduino IDE.

usbFlash

  1. Open zhac.org/flash and find your board.
  2. Click Connect and choose the board's port.
  3. Click Install and wait until it says Done.
  4. Unplug the USB cable, connect Ethernet and power.

updateUpdating later

Open the hub's OTA page and install the newest release over the network. Devices, rules and settings stay. If the new version does not start properly, the hub goes back to the previous one by itself.

Re-flashing over USB? Leave Erase the whole flash first unticked to keep your data.

Stuck? Read Troubleshooting or ask in Discussions.

Open source, local first

The hub firmware and the device library are open source (AGPL-3.0) and work 100% on your own network. Nothing about your home leaves it unless you decide so.

An optional cloud, later

A separate ZHAC Cloud — web console, phone app and remote access — is in private testing. The hub never needs it.
